The IEG Three-Stage Partnership Model

We recommend inverting the traditional TVET hardware-first approach. Online curriculum and digital tools establish credibility, trust, and institutional alignment before any capital equipment is committed — reducing procurement risk for ALAT/TVTC and shortening the path to first enrolment.

Core Principle: Curriculum Before Concrete

The fastest way to fail a TVET centre build is to purchase equipment before instructors know how to use it. IEG's model reverses this: online learning and TTT come first, hardware second. By the time the Model C Cleanroom ships, Saudi instructors are already certified, curriculum is locally adapted, and student intake is scheduled.

Why Online-First Answers ALAT/TVTC's Real Concern

Behind the 10 technical questions is one strategic fear: "What if we build the lab and no one can teach in it?" The online-first model eliminates that risk entirely.

1A

Online Curriculum Package — Delivered Remotely, Month 1–2

IEG delivers the complete BTEC-mapped semiconductor curriculum as bilingual (EN/AR) digital content: 8-process interactive simulator, async video modules for each unit, competency self-assessments, and TVTC occupational standard crosswalk. Saudi instructors can begin learning from Day 1 with zero travel and zero hardware.

USD 20,000 · 100% remote delivery · No equipment required
1B

Korea Technology Tour (TTT Pilot) — 5–7 Days, IEG HQ Seongnam

5–10 ALAT/TVTC instructors visit IEG headquarters. They operate the actual PLC, MPS, and pneumatics equipment that will eventually be installed in Saudi Arabia. They tour Korea Polytechnic University and a smart factory site. On Day 5, they co-design a locally adapted curriculum unit and receive their IEG Certificate of Completion. This is the single highest-leverage activity in the entire roadmap.

USD 27,500 · Includes training fees + accommodation + airport transfer (5–10 pax)
2

Model C Cleanroom Proposal — Submitted at Month 11

Armed with the TTT Pilot Report, IEG submits a full Model C Cleanroom proposal to ALAT procurement. The proposal includes equipment list, layout drawings, commissioning plan, and financing options (EDCF, Saudi-Korea bilateral fund, or direct commercial). The TTT pilot deliverables serve as proof of IEG's delivery capability — making this the lowest-risk major hardware bid ALAT will ever evaluate.

Pricing TBD · EDCF / bilateral financing recommended

IEG Recommendations

Direct answers to ALAT/TVTC's three supplementary questions — stated plainly, based on 15 years of Korean TVET delivery experience.

⭐ Priority 1: Which laboratories should be prioritised for Phase 1?

Do not build any laboratory in Phase 1. The single highest-ROI action in Phase 1 is getting 5–10 Saudi instructors to IEG HQ in Korea. A laboratory without trained instructors is an expensive storage room. The online curriculum (Phase 1A) plus the Korea Technology Tour (Phase 1B) must come first — they produce the certified instructors and the adapted curriculum that will determine exactly which laboratory configuration makes sense for Saudi Arabia. IEG will use the TTT Pilot Report to specify the optimal Model C layout for your specific workforce profile.

⭐ Priority 2: Which digital content should ALAT/TVTC deploy immediately?

The IEG Semiconductor 8-Process Interactive Simulator is available now, at no cost, for ALAT/TVTC internal evaluation. We recommend distributing it to your existing instructors and engineering trainers as a baseline assessment tool. Their performance and engagement with the simulator will help IEG calibrate the Phase 1A curriculum to exactly the right entry level — avoiding both over-pitching and under-pitching the content for your audience.

⭐ Priority 3: Minimum investment to launch Phase 1

USD 20,000 for Phase 1A (online curriculum + simulator). This is the minimum viable investment and delivers tangible, usable deliverables: a complete BTEC mapping table, an interactive digital simulator in EN/AR, and 8 video module scripts. It also gives ALAT/TVTC 60 days of curriculum content to evaluate before committing to the Korea Technology Tour. The full Phase 1 investment (1A + 1B) is USD 47,500 — less than the annual salary of one semiconductor engineer, and it certifies 5–10 instructors who will each train 20+ technicians per year.
