Station 01 · Wafer Preparation

Wafer Preparation & Surface Cleaning

Silicon ingots are sliced, lapped, and chemically cleaned to atomic-level purity. The RCA clean sequence (SC-1 → SC-2) removes organic, ionic, and metallic contaminants that would destroy device yield if left on the surface.

Learning Objectives — Unit 1: Wafer Preparation

▶
BTEC Unit 4039 · NQF Level 3 · NCS 15010201
  • Explain the Czochralski crystal growth process and how crystal orientation (100/111) affects device properties
  • Describe the wire-saw slicing process and specify acceptable warp/bow tolerances for 300mm wafers (±30µm)
  • Perform a two-step RCA clean (SC-1: NH₄OH/H₂O₂/H₂O and SC-2: HCl/H₂O₂/H₂O) and explain the chemistry of each step
  • Calculate surface particle density before and after cleaning and interpret pass/fail against specification
  • Identify four metallic contamination sources (handling, chemicals, equipment) and apply prevention protocols
  • Operate a spin-dry station and confirm wafer cleanliness by water break test (contact angle >70° = hydrophobic pass)

Station 02 · Thermal Oxidation

Thermal Oxidation — SiO₂ Gate Insulator Growth

Silicon wafers are heated in a furnace at 800–1200°C. The Deal-Grove model governs oxide growth: parabolic at thin layers, linear at thick. Gate oxides require dry oxidation for density; field oxides use wet for speed.

Learning Objectives — Unit 2: Thermal Oxidation

▶
BTEC Unit 4039 · NQF Level 3 · NCS 15010201
  • Apply the Deal-Grove model (x² + Ax = B(t+τ)) to predict oxide thickness given temperature and time inputs
  • Distinguish between dry oxidation (Si + O₂ → SiO₂, slow/dense) and wet oxidation (Si + 2H₂O → SiO₂ + 2H₂, fast/porous)
  • Explain why approximately 46% of SiO₂ film thickness sits above the original Si surface while 54% consumes Si
  • Calculate the required furnace time to achieve a target gate oxide thickness of 2.5nm ±0.2nm
  • Identify interface trap density (Dit) as the key quality metric for gate dielectrics and describe how it is minimised
  • Specify post-oxidation anneal conditions (N₂, 950°C, 20 min) and explain the mechanism of H passivation

Station 03 · Photolithography

Photolithography — Pattern Transfer via Light

The Rayleigh resolution limit (R = k₁·λ/NA) defines the smallest printable feature. Manipulate wavelength, numerical aperture, and process factor k₁ to see how resolution, depth of field, and process window change in real time.

Learning Objectives — Unit 3: Photolithography

▶
BTEC Unit 4039 · NQF Level 4 · NCS 15020101
  • Apply the Rayleigh equations R = k₁·λ/NA and DOF = k₂·λ/NA² to calculate resolution and depth of field for given tool parameters
  • Explain the COBD process sequence: HMDS prime → spin coat → soft bake → align/expose → PEB → develop → inspect
  • Compare i-line (365nm), KrF (248nm), ArF (193nm), and EUV (13.5nm) sources by resolution capability and cost
  • Describe how immersion lithography increases effective NA beyond 1.0 by using high-refractive-index fluid
  • Identify the four main photoresist defect modes: scumming, bridging, footing, and T-topping, with root causes
  • Calculate overlay budget for a 4-layer metal stack given alignment tool precision of ±2nm 3σ

Station 04 · Etching

Etching — Material Removal & Pattern Definition

RIE (Reactive Ion Etching) uses RF-powered plasma to etch anisotropically. The etch rate, selectivity, and profile angle are controlled by RF power, chamber pressure, and gas chemistry. Find the optimal window for a given target.

Learning Objectives — Unit 4: Etching

▶
BTEC Unit 4039 · NQF Level 3–4 · NCS 15020201
  • Distinguish isotropic (wet) and anisotropic (dry/plasma) etching and specify when each is appropriate
  • Calculate etch selectivity (S = ER_target / ER_mask) and determine minimum photoresist thickness for a given etch depth
  • Identify the four plasma etch mechanisms: physical sputtering, chemical etching, ion-enhanced chemical, and passivation
  • Explain how increasing RF bias voltage increases ion energy and improves anisotropy at the cost of selectivity
  • Use OES (Optical Emission Spectroscopy) endpoint signal to determine etch completion without over-etching
  • Diagnose etch non-uniformity from a wafer map and identify whether the root cause is gas flow, pressure, or power distribution

Station 05 · Ion Implantation

Ion Implantation — Dopant Introduction by Acceleration

Dopant ions (B, P, As) are accelerated to 10–200 keV and implanted into silicon. Energy controls depth (R_p), dose controls concentration. The resulting Gaussian profile and post-anneal activation determine transistor threshold voltage.

Learning Objectives — Unit 5: Ion Implantation

▶
BTEC Unit 4040 · NQF Level 4 · NCS 15020301
  • Relate ion energy (keV) to projected range R_p using LSS theory and predict implant depth within ±10% accuracy
  • Calculate peak dopant concentration from dose (Φ) and straggle (ΔR_p) using the Gaussian formula N(x) = N_p·exp[-(x-R_p)²/2ΔR_p²]
  • Explain why a 7° tilt angle is applied during implantation and calculate the resulting depth correction factor
  • Specify RTA conditions (temperature, time, ambient) for full dopant activation with <2nm junction depth shift
  • Describe the three stages of implant damage repair: annealing of point defects, recrystallisation, and dopant activation
  • Interpret a SIMS (Secondary Ion Mass Spectrometry) depth profile and extract junction depth and peak concentration values

Station 06 · CVD / Thin Film Deposition

CVD & Thin Films — Chemical Vapour Deposition

Gaseous precursors react at the wafer surface to grow thin films. Temperature, pressure, and gas flow govern deposition rate, step coverage, and film stress. Tune the recipe for your target film type and observe uniformity impact.

Learning Objectives — Unit 6: CVD / Thin Film Deposition

▶
BTEC Unit 4040 · NQF Level 4 · NCS 15020401
  • Compare LPCVD, PECVD, APCVD, and ALD by temperature, pressure, conformality, and typical applications
  • Derive the Arrhenius-type deposition rate equation and predict rate change for a 50°C temperature increase
  • Define step coverage and calculate the aspect-ratio-dependent fill factor for a 4:1 trench using PECVD parameters
  • Specify an ALD (Atomic Layer Deposition) recipe cycle sequence for HfO₂ gate dielectric deposition at sub-2nm thickness
  • Measure and interpret film stress (compressive vs. tensile) from wafer bow data using Stoney's equation
  • Identify WERR (Wet Etch Rate Ratio) as a quality metric and specify the acceptance criterion for LPCVD SiO₂ (<2× thermal oxide)

Station 07 · Metallisation

Metallisation — Interconnect & Contact Formation

Copper damascene wiring connects transistors into circuits across 10–15+ metal layers. CMP (Chemical Mechanical Planarisation) removes overburden and achieves global flatness. Adjust slurry, pressure, and time to hit the target removal.

Learning Objectives — Unit 7: Metallisation & CMP

▶
BTEC Unit 4040 · NQF Level 4 · NCS 15020501
  • Explain why copper replaced aluminium as the primary interconnect metal and quantify the resistivity advantage (Cu 1.68 vs Al 2.65 µΩ·cm)
  • Describe the dual-damascene process sequence: via etch → trench etch → TaN/Ta barrier PVD → Cu seed PVD → Cu ECD → CMP
  • Apply Preston's equation (RR = k_p · P · v) to calculate Cu CMP removal rate for given platen pressure and velocity
  • Explain the role of the TaN diffusion barrier in preventing Cu migration into SiO₂ and Si junction regions
  • Identify the three CMP failure modes: dishing, erosion, and micro-scratch, and specify the process conditions that cause each
  • Calculate interconnect RC delay for a given Cu line geometry and low-k dielectric constant and explain its impact on circuit speed

Station 08 · Testing & Packaging

Testing & Packaging — Yield Economics & Cost Per Die

Yield is the ultimate scorecard of the entire fabrication process. Adjust wafer size, die size, and defect density to calculate gross die, yield, and cost per good die. This is how semiconductor economics are built.

Learning Objectives — Unit 8: Testing, Yield & Packaging

▶
BTEC Unit 4040 · NQF Level 4 · NCS 15020601
  • Apply the Murphy yield model Y = [1 – exp(–D₀A)] / (D₀A) to calculate die yield from defect density and die area
  • Calculate gross die per wafer (GDW) using GDW = π·(d/2)² / A – π·d / √(2A) and explain the edge exclusion penalty
  • Compute cost per good die = (wafer cost / GDW) / yield and perform sensitivity analysis on each variable
  • Interpret a wafer map showing clustered vs. random failures and identify the process stage most likely responsible
  • Describe the BEOL assembly sequence: probe test → thin → dice → DAF → wire bond/flip-chip → mould → FT → burn-in
  • Compare BGA, QFN, CSP, and 3D-IC packaging formats by pin count, thermal resistance, and use case
