Station 01 · Wafer Preparation

Wafer Preparation
& Surface Cleaning

The foundation of all semiconductor manufacturing. A silicon ingot is sliced, lapped, and chemically cleaned to atomic-level purity before any process begins.

IEG REF
Incheon Semiconductor High School — IEG supplied the complete 8-process semiconductor testbed, including wafer preparation and inspection stations, enabling students to handle actual 300mm Si wafers in a controlled educational cleanroom environment.
Key Process Steps
  • Crystal growing (Czochralski method)
  • Ingot slicing with wire saw (±5µm precision)
  • Lapping & edge grinding
  • RCA cleaning (SC-1 + SC-2 chemical bath)
  • DI water rinse + spin-dry
  • Surface inspection (particle count < 0.3)
Common Defect Modes
  • Metallic contamination → HF dip required
  • Organic residue → piranha clean (H₂SO₄/H₂O₂)
  • Scratches → rewire saw or reject
  • Warp / bow → affects lithography alignment
  • Particle adhesion → ultrasonic bath treatment

Assessment: What is the primary purpose of RCA cleaning in wafer preparation?
✓ RCA cleaning (developed at RCA Laboratories) uses a two-step chemical bath — SC-1 (NH₄OH/H₂O₂/H₂O) removes organics and particles, SC-2 (HCl/H₂O₂/H₂O) removes metallic ions. This establishes the contamination-free baseline that all subsequent processes depend on.
Station 02 · Thermal Oxidation

Thermal Oxidation
SiO₂ Gate Insulator Growth

Silicon wafers are heated in a furnace at 800–1200°C. Silicon atoms react with O₂ or H₂O to form a thermally grown SiO₂ layer — the most critical insulator in MOSFET fabrication.

IEG REF
Korea Polytechnic University, Asan Campus — IEG delivered the Semiconductor Photolithography Equipment suite, which includes the pre-lithography oxidation furnace station. Students observe real Deal-Grove oxide growth kinetics on 200mm wafers.
Dry vs. Wet Oxidation
  • Dry (O₂): slow, dense, high quality — gate oxide
  • Wet (H₂O steam): fast, lower density — field oxide
  • Deal-Grove model governs growth kinetics
  • Approximately 54% of oxide grows into Si wafer
Critical Control Parameters
  • Temperature: 800–1200°C (±0.5°C precision)
  • Gas flow rate: O₂ or H₂O vapour (slpm)
  • Time: seconds (thin gate) to hours (field)
  • Ambient pressure: atmospheric or reduced
  • Post-oxide anneal in N₂ for interface quality

Assessment: Which oxidation method produces higher-quality, denser oxide for use as a gate insulator?
✓ Dry oxidation produces a denser, higher-quality SiO₂ layer with fewer defects at the Si/SiO₂ interface, making it the preferred method for gate insulators despite its slower growth rate. Wet oxidation is faster but produces a more porous oxide suited to thick field isolation layers.
Station 03 · Photolithography

Photolithography
Pattern Transfer via Light

The defining process of semiconductor manufacturing. Circuit patterns on a photomask are projected onto a photoresist-coated wafer, transferred with nanometre precision using UV or EUV light.

IEG REF
Korea Polytechnic University, Asan Campus — IEG's dedicated Semiconductor Photolithography Equipment set covers coating, exposure, bake, and develop (COBD) sequences. Trainers use a mask aligner with UV lamp to expose real photoresist patterns on training wafers.
COBD Process Sequence
  • HMDS prime — improves PR adhesion to oxide
  • Spin coat — 3000–5000 rpm → 1µm film
  • Soft bake (PEB) — 90°C, 60s to evaporate solvent
  • Alignment & exposure — mask aligner, UV 365nm
  • Post-exposure bake (PEB) — 110°C, activates acid
  • Development — positive or negative PR removal
Resolution Limit (Rayleigh)
  • R = k₁ × λ / NA
  • i-line (365nm): ~350nm resolution
  • KrF (248nm): ~130nm resolution
  • ArF (193nm): ~65nm (with immersion)
  • EUV (13.5nm): <7nm (cutting-edge nodes)

Assessment: In the Rayleigh resolution equation R = k₁·λ/NA, which change would MOST improve (reduce) the minimum printable feature size?
✓ Resolution improves (smaller R) when λ decreases or NA increases. The industry has pursued both — moving from 365nm → 248nm → 193nm → 13.5nm (EUV), while increasing NA to 1.35 (immersion) and now >0.55 (High-NA EUV). k₁ has also been reduced through OPC and multiple patterning techniques.
Station 04 · Etching

Etching
Material Removal & Pattern Definition

After lithography defines the pattern in photoresist, etching transfers it into underlying layers — oxide, metal, or silicon — using wet chemical solutions or plasma (dry etching).

IEG REF
Korea Semiconductor Academy — IEG supplied Plasma Equipment for process control and semiconductor etch training. Trainees operate a parallel-plate RIE chamber, setting RF power, gas flow, and pressure parameters to achieve target etch profiles.
Wet vs. Dry Etch
  • Wet: isotropic, high selectivity, low cost, >100nm
  • Dry (RIE): anisotropic, vertical profiles, <10nm capable
  • Selectivity = etch rate of target ÷ mask material
  • Endpoint detection by OES (optical emission spectroscopy)
Etch Rate Control
  • RF power — ion energy & plasma density
  • Gas chemistry — CF₄, Cl₂, HBr, SF₆
  • Chamber pressure — mean free path of ions
  • Temperature — controls chemical reactivity
  • Bias voltage — directionality of ion bombardment

Assessment: Why is dry (plasma) etching preferred over wet etching for sub-micron feature patterning?
✓ Dry etching uses directional ion bombardment to etch preferentially in the vertical direction. This anisotropy is essential when feature widths are smaller than the etch depth — wet chemical etching undercuts the mask equally in all directions, destroying the pattern integrity at sub-micron scales.
Station 05 · Ion Implantation

Ion Implantation
Dopant Introduction by Acceleration

Dopant ions (boron, phosphorus, arsenic) are accelerated to 10–200keV and shot into the silicon wafer. The depth and concentration are precisely controlled by energy and dose — far more precise than diffusion.

IEG REF
Gangwon Technopark — IEG supplied Semiconductor Maintenance Equipment covering the doping and implantation stages. Engineers learn to calibrate dose-energy relationships and interpret SIMS (Secondary Ion Mass Spectrometry) dopant profiles.
Key Parameters
  • Dose (ions/cm²) — controls carrier concentration
  • Energy (keV) — controls implant depth (Rp)
  • Tilt angle — reduces channelling effects
  • Species: B (p-type), P / As (n-type)
  • Post-implant RTA to activate dopants & repair lattice
Advantages vs. Diffusion
  • Precise depth control via energy selection
  • Precise dose control via beam current × time
  • Low-temperature process — no dopant redistribution
  • Works through thin oxide or nitride masks
  • Essential for retrograde wells and halo implants

Assessment: What is the purpose of Rapid Thermal Annealing (RTA) after ion implantation?
✓ Ion implantation physically damages the crystal lattice (amorphisation) and leaves dopant atoms in interstitial (inactive) positions. RTA at ~900–1000°C for 10–60 seconds repairs the crystal structure and moves dopant atoms onto lattice sites where they electrically activate — becoming electrically active carriers (holes or electrons).
Station 06 · CVD / Thin Film Deposition

CVD & Thin Films
Chemical Vapour Deposition

Gaseous precursors react at the wafer surface to deposit thin films of dielectrics, polysilicon, or metals. CVD enables conformal, pinhole-free coverage at nanometre scale — impossible with physical methods alone.

IEG REF
Korea Semiconductor Academy — IEG's Process Control Operation Software Testing Equipment allows trainees to simulate and monitor PECVD (Plasma-Enhanced CVD) recipes digitally before running on actual chambers, reducing material waste and training risk.
CVD Variants
  • LPCVD: 0.1–1 Torr, 550–900°C — best conformality
  • PECVD: plasma activation, low temp (<400°C)
  • APCVD: atmospheric, fast rate, less conformal
  • ALD: atomic layer, 1 monolayer/cycle, <400°C
  • HDP-CVD: high-density plasma, gap-fill capability
Film Quality Metrics
  • Thickness uniformity: <±1% across 300mm wafer
  • Step coverage: >90% in high-AR trenches
  • Refractive index (ellipsometry measurement)
  • Wet etch rate ratio (WERR) vs. thermal oxide
  • Stress: compressive vs. tensile (wafer bow)

Assessment: Why is Atomic Layer Deposition (ALD) preferred over LPCVD for depositing high-k gate dielectrics at advanced nodes?
✓ ALD alternates self-limiting surface reactions: each precursor pulse deposits exactly one monolayer before purging, ensuring perfect thickness control and >99% step coverage in high-aspect-ratio features. At 2–3nm high-k dielectric thicknesses (HfO₂, Al₂O₃), even one atomic layer of variation is unacceptable — only ALD achieves this.
Station 07 · Metallisation

Metallisation
Interconnect & Contact Formation

Metal layers connect transistors into circuits. Modern chips have 10–15+ metal layers of copper damascene wiring, each separated by low-k dielectrics and linked through tungsten or cobalt contacts and vias.

IEG REF
Korea Polytechnic University, Incheon Campus — IEG supplied Semiconductor Packaging Equipment covering back-end metallisation and contact formation. Trainees learn PVD sputtering parameters and CMP endpoint control using IEG's simulation-linked training modules.
Copper Damascene Process
  • Etch trenches/vias in low-k dielectric (ILD)
  • PVD TaN/Ta barrier layer deposition
  • PVD Cu seed layer
  • Electrochemical deposition (ECD) — fills trenches
  • CMP — removes overburden, planarises surface
Why Copper Replaced Aluminium
  • Cu resistivity: 1.68 µΩ·cm vs Al 2.65 µΩ·cm
  • Better electromigration resistance (longer lifetime)
  • Lower RC delay at advanced nodes
  • Challenge: Cu diffuses into Si → TaN barrier needed
  • Now: Co, Ru replacing Cu at <5nm nodes

Assessment: Why is a TaN/Ta barrier layer deposited before copper in the damascene process?
✓ Copper is a fast diffuser in silicon and SiO₂. Even trace amounts cause deep-level traps and junction leakage, destroying MOSFET performance. The TaN diffusion barrier (deposited by PVD) is chemically inert and prevents Cu migration, while the Ta wetting layer improves Cu seed adhesion for ECD fill.
Station 08 · Testing & Packaging

Testing & Packaging
Wafer Probe, Dicing & Assembly

The final stage converts fabricated wafers into individual packaged chips ready for the market. Electrical testing identifies good dies (yield management), followed by dicing, wire bonding or flip-chip, and encapsulation.

Wafer probe yield >95% Package test yield >99% Overall fab yield 80–95%
IEG REF
Korea Polytechnic University, Incheon Campus — IEG's Semiconductor Packaging Equipment covers wafer probe testing, dicing saw operation, wire bonding, and final test (FT) simulation. Trainees generate and interpret wafer maps and calculate die yield using actual test data from IEG training wafers.
Back-End-of-Line (BEOL) Sequence
  • Wafer probe test — electrical sort (E-sort)
  • Wafer thinning (grinding to <100µm)
  • Dicing — blade, laser, or plasma saw
  • Die attach — epoxy or eutectic bond to substrate
  • Wire bonding or flip-chip bumping
  • Mould compound (EMC) encapsulation
  • Final test (FT) + burn-in at elevated temperature
Package Types
  • BGA — Ball Grid Array (high pin count)
  • QFN — Quad Flat No-lead (compact)
  • CSP — Chip Scale Package (mobile)
  • 3D-IC — die stacking with TSV (HBM memory)
  • CoWoS — CoW on Substrate (AI accelerators)

Assessment: What does a wafer map showing clustered failing dies near the wafer edge most likely indicate?
✓ Clustered edge failures are a strong signature of process uniformity problems. CVD tools may have non-uniform gas flow at the wafer edge; CMP pads wear non-uniformly; lithography steppers may have edge-of-field aberrations. A random scatter pattern suggests particle contamination; a repeating pattern across the wafer indicates a reticle or process recipe defect.
