KMSTTH proposal timeline and decision gates for IEG

The proposal should move only through staged validation. The practical goal is not to approve a broad partnership now, but to test whether KMSTTH has enough legal, financial, and institutional substance to justify a narrowly scoped pilot.

Core judgment

Signal interest, not commitment. IEG should keep the conversation constructive while tying every next step to documentation, counterpart verification, and a one-country pilot structure in a lower-risk Gulf market.

What KMSTTH appears to gain first

Immediate credibility, operating substance, training systems, and partner confidence through association with IEG.

What IEG can gain only if validated

Access to institutional channels, donor-backed pilots, and a new regional platform that converts into funded execution.

Decision code
GO · proceed to pilot HOLD · request proof NO-GO · stop escalation
Stage 1 · today to this week

Initial validation meeting

Separate vision from substance before IEG lends meaningful time, brand value, or implementation attention.

Immediate actions
Decision gate
Proceed only if KMSTTH can identify its legal form, current stage, and intended operating structure.
Hold if answers remain high-level and documents are promised later.
No-go if the entity, leadership authority, or ownership structure is unclear.
Stage 2 · next 2 to 4 weeks

Document review and risk screening

Use written materials to test whether the proposal is operationally real or still mostly narrative.

What to review
Decision gate
Proceed if funding logic, partner claims, and governance basics survive document review.
Hold if the materials are incomplete but potentially recoverable.
No-go if core claims cannot be evidenced or if high-risk geography dominates the near-term plan.
Stage 3 · 3 to 6 months

Pilot structuring, not broad partnership

Translate interest into a controlled test case with hard boundaries on scope, cost, and representation.

Preferred structure
Decision gate
Proceed if the pilot has named responsibilities, budget logic, and controlled brand use.
Hold if the pilot scope is still too broad or if cost-sharing remains undefined.
No-go if KMSTTH expects open-ended technical support without formal structure.
Stage 4 · after 6 months

Scale only after proof of execution

Regional expansion should follow evidence, not presentation ambition.

Proof required
Decision gate
Expand only if the pilot proves KMSTTH can convert network claims into funded delivery.
Hold if pilot performance is mixed or market conditions remain unstable.
No-go on regional rollout if the platform remains dependent mainly on IEG’s credibility.

Decision points that matter most

  • Legal reality: Is KMSTTH an actual legal counterpart yet?
  • Funding credibility: Who is paying for the first phase, and is that money real?
  • Partner substance: Which relationships are signed, not just named?
  • IEG role clarity: Is IEG lending expertise selectively, or being asked to carry execution?
  • Geographic discipline: Can the proposal stay in lower-risk Gulf markets first?

Best closing line for today

“We see potential in the concept. The next practical step for us would be to review the supporting documents and then determine whether there is a well-defined pilot opportunity that makes sense for both sides.”
